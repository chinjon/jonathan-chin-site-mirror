a year ago i landed on a book review video from the new york times. i wasn’t particularly interested or even seeking out this video but, as i was watching the video, a strange fear crept along the back of my mind. i conceded immediately i would not be able to read this book from front to back. 

the [reading crisis that has been discussed ad nauseam](https://archive.ph/https://www.theatlantic.com/magazine/2026/08/reading-crisis-postliterate-age/687618/) for more than a decade, with both anecdotal and studies narrating the decline. i wasn’t the most voracious reader, but i could finish a book, eventually. last year i started reading again, but in spurts, mostly using audiobooks. this year i treated my reading problem with a bit more urgency to great success.

here's what i did to get back into reading, this may or may not work for you:

### watch book recommendation videos

ironically, the thing that helped my reading get kicked back into overdrive was looking up book recommendations videos online. reading reviews and surveying ratings is one thing, but listening to people get excited about books got me more invested. the content creators i’ve used to find books:

- [life on books](https://www.youtube.com/@Lifeonbooks)
- [swift busboy manuvers](https://www.youtube.com/@swiftbusboymaneuvers)
- [vanisha marie](https://www.youtube.com/@Vanishamarie)

### get an app

what isn’t an application these days. the trick was finding a goodreads alternative, which there was plenty, but hard to filter out what was good. this isn’t all that necessary, but it helped me catalog books i wanted to read and helped track my reading progress for the week/year. i recommend book tracker for reading stats, its non-social with good tools to track your reading progress, even tracks how much you spent on books. for social i use storygraph, but it’s mostly to look up reviews and catalog books i want to read. you can also set up a reading streak to sort of game-ify your reading habit.

- [book tracker](https://booktrack.app/)
- [the storygraph](https://app.thestorygraph.com/)
- [find new books](https://www.meetnewbooks.com/notabot.html?return_url=%2F)

### go buy a book

i love physical books, much easier to read, carry and makes me want to read more. i suggested picking up used books since they’re cheaper (especially from used book stores), but definitely buy new to support authors you enjoy. i also started taking notes to be more engaged in the books i read. this is not a necessary step, but i’ve found it has made it easier for me. i use index cards to jot down quotes and analysis with page numbers.

### go to the library

an obvious place, but also surprisingly its lesser known of the digital resources libraries provide like kanopy. overdrive or libby are the bigger platforms library systems.

### slim down your reading format

<img class="blog-image" src="https://file.garden/akFLbgUm9G3LrWhE/IMG_9201.jpeg" alt="screen shot of reading stats from book tracker app" />

i personally don’t use e-readers that much, i did buy a small one recently and it’s helped. the xteink x4 is a great little e-reader that lets you install custom firmware. a huge community has grown around it and theres plenty of resources for custom firmware. you can also get some nice formatted public domain books online. given the viral nature of the xteink e-readers, a few other e-ink companies are releasing smaller e-reader devices as well.

- [xteink x4](https://www.xteink.com/products/xteink-x4)
- [crosspoint reader](https://crosspointreader.com/)

### take reading in small strides

reading is very much like exercise, you need to build up strength, form and stamina. start small, make a goal of reading 10, 5 or even just 1 page a day. quantity doesn’t matter, its just the consistency needs to be built. read on your commute, with your breakfast, or right before bed. read any time, if you can spend more than 5 minutes on your phone, you can read.

## my progress

<img class="blog-image" src="https://file.garden/akFLbgUm9G3LrWhE/book-tracker" alt="screen shot of reading stats from book tracker app" />

i initially started this drive to read in april of 2026, skeptical that i could complete any book. setting a goal of 10 books for 2026, not being sure of my consistency and devotion to reading. however, i'm happy to say i've exceed that goal, as of july 2026, with 16 books completed on the year. i have also spent nearly $500 on books, and $70 on my little e-reader. one side effect is that now i have too many books, but also motivates me to keep reading.
